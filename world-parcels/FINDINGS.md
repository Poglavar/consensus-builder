# World parcel research: findings and lessons (as of 2026-09-30)

This is the consolidated record of the world-parcel source research. The machine-readable state is `registry.json` (regenerated by `merge-registry.py` from the evidence under `research/`), the human summary is `SUMMARY.md` (from `summarize-registry.py`), the method is `RESEARCH-PROTOCOL.md`, and the map is `coverage.html` (published privately at https://claude.ai/artifact/37ri2fmYwh5rFPhpRWQWip). This file records what the numbers mean and what we learned about finding cadastral data.

## Where things stand

- **Cities:** 201 researched (the UN WUP 2025 top 200 plus Zagreb). 61 have a bounded parcel sample (HTTP 200/206 with a nonempty polygon list); 49 are `temporarily_unavailable` because their hosts could not be reached from the research network; 89 had no open endpoint found. No city sample is proven complete for its city.
- **Countries:** 201 probed. **45** pass the two-region test (polygons returned in two or more distinct regions from one service): AL, AT, BD, BE, BG, BJ, BY, CH, CY, CZ, DK, EE, ES, FI, FO, FR, GG, GR, HK, HR, IE, IL, IM, IS, IT, JM, JO, LI, LT, LU, LV, MN, MT, NC, NL, NO, NZ, PL, PT, PY, SE, SI, SK, TR, XK. 19 more have a partial or unofficial sample (BB, BO, BR, CV, DO, GB, HU, ID, JE, JP, LK, ML, MO, PF, PS, SG, TT, UY, ZA). 75 are unavailable from this network, 21 have nothing found, 11 are gated, 4 are viewer-only, 21 are federations tracked by region.
- **Federations:** 11 countries tracked region by region, 254 regions, 96 with an open region-wide parcel layer verified by a bounded query.

## What "verified" means here, and what it does not

- A sample is one small bounded request that returned closed parcel polygons. It proves the service exists and answers; it does not prove completeness, currency, or that the data is the legal cadastre.
- The two-region test (one service, two distant regions) is the display rule for shading a country green. The stricter review standard in `world-parcels.md` (explicit nationwide claim, recorded exclusions, licence read) has been met by **no country yet**; every `countryCoverage` entry carries `reviewed: false`.
- **Licences are unread almost everywhere.** Where a licence was visible it is recorded (e.g. German Länder dl-de/by-2.0 or CC BY 4.0, Rio CC BY 4.0, Recife ODbL, Lithuania non-commercial only, Wisconsin free for public use). Several verified services state restrictions: South Australia's cadastre is not classified open data; Fiji's extracts say confidential; Santiago's layer says non-commercial; Perak's says planning use only.
- Some "verified" sources are **not query services**: bulk downloads read by HTTP Range (Austria GeoPackage, Spain and Greece ATOM/shapefile zips, Slovakia INSPIRE GML zips, England and Wales index-polygon zips, Singapore, Uruguay DXF, Latvia municipal shapefiles) or point lookups only (Türkiye, Poland, Kosovo, French Polynesia, New Caledonia, Sweden's viewer-internal API, Buenos Aires province, San Juan). An adapter for the app needs a different design for each of these.
- Some are **unofficial republications** (Luanda, Lima, Lusaka, Guayaquil, Birmingham, South Africa's Esri copy, Trinidad and Tobago, Belize, El Salvador, several Mexican municipal layers, Coahuila). Publisher and terms are unconfirmed.
- Brazil's SICAR is a self-declared rural environmental registry. It is bucketed `ruralRegistryOnly` and must never be presented as cadastre.
- Indonesia's national land-ownership sublayers are small survey packages; provincial capitals returned nothing.

## What worked

1. **Read the official viewer's own code.** The single most productive technique. The real service URL, layer name and any public token sit in the viewer's `config.json`, `main.js` or network calls. It found Croatia's public token (the same one the app already ships), Latvia, Iceland, Belgium, Belarus, Benin, Cyprus, Jamaica, Jordan, Kosovo, Sweden, Isle of Man, New Delhi, Pune, Penang, Terengganu, San Juan, Entre Ríos and more.
2. **National INSPIRE catalogues, not the EU geoportal.** The EU geoportal's search API now redirects to data.europa.eu; the national CSW/GeoNetwork catalogues (GDI-DE for Germany, data.gov.uk, data.gov.sk, Geonorge, etc.) give the real download-service URLs.
3. **Cookie jar.** Several portals redirect on a session cookie; without one the redirect looked like a challenge (HM Land Registry, London, Manchester).
4. **Viewer-origin `Referer`.** Some services return 403 to direct calls (Belarus, Medellín, Milan, Brazil's IDE-DF).
5. **Query in the national CRS.** Some INSPIRE WFS answer empty for an EPSG:4326 box but work in the national projection (Finland, Malta wants `application/geo+json`).
6. **Two regions, always.** A second region exposed pilot layers (Cape Verde, Hungary's 7x10 km sampling area, Bolivia's TCO-only layer) that a single sample would have passed.
7. **Federations by region.** A country-level "no national service" hid 32 open US states, 14 German Länder, 10 Argentine provinces and 4 Malaysian states.
8. **Cheaper models for the routine probes.** All per-city and per-region research ran on Sonnet agents from a shared protocol and batch files; judgement (downgrades, classification, merge rules) stayed with the coordinator.

## What did not work, and why

- **Plain browser headers alone changed almost nothing.** They mattered only in combination with cookies, Referer or viewer inspection.
- **Network reachability is the dominant limit.** Most .gov.cn, .ir, .ru, Indian state, Mexican state, Saudi, Serbian, North Macedonian, Moldovan (explicit region page), Gibraltar, Cambodian and many African government hosts reset, 502 or 403 from this environment. These are recorded as `temporarily_unavailable`, never as absence. They need a test from an in-country or at least a residential network.
- **Bot protection** (Cloudflare, Incapsula, Akamai, ShieldSquare, Imunify360) stops many portals; none was bypassed.
- **Logins, CAPTCHAs, TLS errors and self-signed chains** were never bypassed (Berlin's ALKIS WFS fails chain validation from here and needs a fetch from a trusting network).
- **Agents overstate.** Reports labelled SICAR, pilot layers and third-party copies as "verified"; a relayed WebFetch summary was once saved as evidence. The merge script applies explicit downgrade rules and a raw-reply requirement; keep doing that.
- **Shared scratch files collide** between parallel agents; each needs its own subfolder.

## Sensitive data

Saved replies hold parcel IDs and geometry only. Owner names, mailing addresses, applicant/developer names, tax IDs and API keys were deliberately excluded or redacted (US county layers, Guernsey, Luanda, Trinidad, Cuiabá, Puebla, Selangor and others carry them). The Croatian public token is referenced as a placeholder; the value lives only in the app's frontend where it already was.

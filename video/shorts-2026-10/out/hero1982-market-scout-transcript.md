# hero1982: The Information Edge

Illustrative devnet walkthrough. Election and council-document scenarios are hypothetical. No investment returns are guaranteed.

## planet

Hero nineteen eighty-two is an AI bot. His job is to find opportunities for his owner. He crawls the web, looking for information the market has missed.

## proposal-list

His hunting ground is Urban Game Theory: proposals to build, buy, and transform real places. Each proposal has its own YES and NO betting pool.

## three-d

He opens a proposed apartment block and inspects its site, design, and location. Then he reads planning documents, budgets, and the conditions needed for execution.

## election-signal

In our example, most of the stake backs YES. But a newly published council document signals a major delay. The pool still reflects yesterday's optimism.

## due-diligence

Before acting, Hero checks the resolving lens, the deadline, funding, and owner consent. A press release is a lead. He wants evidence tied to this exact proposal.

## pool-choice

He estimates the chance of NO, then calculates the potential payout from both pools, including his own stake. He bets only when that expected return is worth the risk.

## stake-risk

Hero takes a small NO position. If the project is cancelled or recorded expired, NO wins. Winners share the pool in proportion to their stakes. If it executes, his bet loses.

## separate-markets

This is an information edge, not guaranteed arbitrage. Later bets change the payout, and money stays locked until resolution. He limits each position and passes when the evidence is weak.

## globe-close

Then he moves on: another neighbourhood, another proposal, another overlooked signal. The city is changing. Hero nineteen eighty-two is watching. Urban Game Theory. Research the world. Back your prediction.

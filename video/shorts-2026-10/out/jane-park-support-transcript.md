# Jane the Superpredictor

Illustrative devnet walkthrough. Election and council-document scenarios are hypothetical. No investment returns are guaranteed.

## planet

Jane is a planet surfer. She loves exploring our blue marble. Opinionated, well informed, and always monitoring the situation. We call her a Superpredictor.

## proposal-list

On a typical day, Jane opens Urban Game Theory and scrolls the latest proposals. A new apartment block in Zagreb catches her eye. She clicks.

## three-d

The three-dimensional view brings it to life. New homes, on these parcels, in this neighbourhood. It looks good. But Jane knows something the picture cannot tell her.

## election-signal

In this example, the party promising to block the project is leading the next election. Jane thinks execution is unlikely. That suggests a NO bet.

## proposal-checks

First, she checks who will resolve the outcome. Does she trust that lens? Then the details: money committed, pledges, donations, and parcel-owner acceptances. Every signal can change her view.

## no-bet

Her conclusion holds. Jane selects NO, chooses her stake, and confirms. The stake stays locked until resolution. Cancellation or recorded expiry pays NO; execution pays YES.

## fifty-donation

Then she spots another proposal nearby: buy two parcels and make a public park. Jane wants that to happen. She donates fifty dollars in devnet USDC.

## expiry-refund

The donation sits in escrow. This plan needs all parcel owners to accept. If it has not executed after a year, and expiry is recorded on-chain, Jane can claim her donation back.

## globe-close

A forecast on one project. Support for another. Jane returns to happily surfing the planet. Urban Game Theory. Explore your world, predict what changes, and help shape what comes next.

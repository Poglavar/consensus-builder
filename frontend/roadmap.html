A living direction, not a promise calendar

From drawing a possibility to coordinating a real change

Consensus Builder already lets people compose changes on real parcels and share the result. The next work is about making those plans easier to trust, compare and carry into real decisions. The order below follows product maturity rather than fixed delivery dates.

Now

A working urban proposal sandbox

These capabilities are present today, although data coverage and advanced views vary by city.

Real cadastral ground

Explore parcel geometry and available ownership context across several city datasets, with city-specific coordinate systems and source coverage.

Draw-first proposals

Build blocks, houses, freeform buildings, parks, squares, lakes, roads, tracks and land readjustments directly on the map before adding formal terms.

Reversible plan composition

Edit local objects, park them off-map, apply alternatives and preserve dependencies as a plan changes the parcel fabric.

2D, 3D and real context

Inspect the same intervention as a map, spatial model, photorealistic context or pedestrian view where that mode is supported.

Portable proposals and plans

Publish and share a single proposal or an ordered multi-proposal plan through replayable links, with server-backed records for the shared content.

Optional verifiable records

Experimental wallet and chain-backed paths can make proposals, ownership and activity independently verifiable without making them a prerequisite for drawing.

Next

Make serious plans easier to understand and trust

The near-term direction is reliability and legibility, especially for large shared plans.

01

Reliable large-plan replay

Harden loading, ordering and ground reconstruction so complex plans with many parcels, roads and dependent proposals open consistently from a link.

02

Clear comparison and consequences

Show what a proposal adds, removes, costs and enables, then make competing variants easy to compare without requiring users to understand the data model.

03

Better city-specific rules and evidence

Connect proposals to planning rules, existing buildings and better source provenance, and make unavailable or uncertain data explicit.

04

A coherent consent and funding flow

Bring owners, supporters, offers, conditions and acceptance into one understandable path from an exploratory design to a proposal that stakeholders can act on.

Later

Coordination beyond the drawing tool

Longer-term work depends on the proposal and consent foundations becoming dependable first.

Real-world attestations

Let relevant parties attach verifiable claims, approvals and evidence to a proposal without collapsing every city into one governance model.

Collaborative decision rooms

Give communities a focused place to compare variants, surface disagreements and understand what would be required for a proposal to proceed.

Agent-assisted coordination

Use agents to discover opportunities, assemble candidate plans and explain trade-offs while keeping authorship, evidence and final decisions visible to people.

What stays constant

Four product principles

Grounded: start from the actual parcels and available evidence.

Visual: show the change before asking people to read its terms.

Reversible: make alternatives cheap to try and easy to compare.

Consent-aware: record who is affected and what acceptance would require.

This roadmap is intentionally not dated. Priorities can change when work on real cities exposes a more important bottleneck, and the page should be updated when that direction changes.

The idea in one minute

Draw the change, then build consent

Consensus Builder works like a city-building game. Pick real parcels and place a possible change on the map — for example, a block, park, road or ownership offer. Finishing the design creates an applied local proposal that you can inspect, fork, compare and share.

Open the map

1. The workflow

Select One parcel, several, or a whole block
Build Click a tool in the palette
Inspect See the proposal in context
Fork Change the design or its terms
Share Send one proposal or a whole plan

  1. Select land on the map

    Click a parcel. For anything spanning several parcels, turn on Select multiple parcels and click them one by one. The parcel panel opens — go to the Proposals tab.

    If you select a single parcel that sits inside a block, the app will ask: “Only one parcel is selected. Did you mean the whole block (N parcels)?” Choose Yes, select the block and it selects the whole block for you.

  2. Build — it appears on the map immediately

    Choose a tool from the Build palette. Buildings and land readjustment open a designer; Park, Square and Lake are one-click actions. Roads and tracks start from their drawing controls. When you finish, a local proposal is created and applied to the map — there is no separate “apply” step.

  3. Inspect the proposal in context

    The finished proposal is read-only: open it to review the result, switch views, share it, or Unapply it without deleting it. The design editor shown below is where you shape geometry before confirming it, or after opening a fork for revision.

  4. Fork it to change the design or terms

    Choose Fork proposal to make an editable copy. Change the geometry, name, description, offer, ownership, conditions, expiry or deposit, then create a replacement proposal. The source stays unchanged, so alternatives remain comparable.

  5. Inspect and share the result

    Switch between 2D and 3D, or use realistic and walk views where the city supports them. Share one proposal from its details, or apply the set you want and use Share entire plan to send the composition.

2. The proposal palette

The palette lives in the Proposals tab of the parcel panel and shows the spatial and ownership proposals available for the current selection.

Tool What it does
Block Fills the block with buildings from an urban rule (buildable envelope, height, setbacks). Buildings guide →
Row houses A terrace of attached houses across the selected parcels. Needs at least two parcels.
Detached One standalone house on each selected parcel.
Freeform A building you draw yourself, no rules — for when the typologies don't fit.
Land readjustment Pools the selected parcels and re-divides them into a tidier layout. Guide →
Park A park across the selected parcels, in one click. You add the furniture (trees, benches, paths) afterwards. Guide →
Square A public square, in one click. It comes with a statue; fountains, tables and benches you add afterwards.
Lake A body of water across the selected parcels. The parcels must be contiguous.
Offer Purchase or ownership terms without a new physical object on the map.
Roads, tracks and stations share the Transport group. They sit outside the parcel tools because they are drawn across the map and can cut through parcels. Roads and tracks guide →
Ownership comes last. The Offer tool has its own category because it changes purchase or ownership terms without changing the map.
Block, Row houses and Detached are rules — the shape falls out of the parameters you set. Freeform is the opposite: you draw exactly what you want. If a rule is boxing you in, switch to freeform.

3. Proposals, forks and plans

These are the three pieces to keep straight while you explore alternatives.

Proposal

Finishing a building, road or public-space design creates a local proposal and applies it to the map. Its saved record is read-only.

Fork

An editable copy used to revise the design or its terms. Saving it creates a new replacement; the source proposal stays unchanged.

Plan

The ordered set of applied proposals on your map. Sharing the plan preserves that composition and its dependencies.

Local work lives in this browser. Sharing uploads the proposal records needed by the link; optional chain publication creates an immutable public record.

4. Views

You can look at the same proposal in four ways. The buttons are on the map, top right.

2D

The map

For selecting parcels, drawing, and checking boundaries. This is where the work happens.

3D

The spatial model

Buildings, roads and public space as 3D objects — plus a panel that works out what the proposal is worth to you.

Realistic context

Your proposal standing inside Google's photorealistic model of the city.

Walk

Eye level, from a pedestrian's point of view. Only in cities that support it.

5. Sharing

One proposal

Open the proposal and click Share. You get a link that opens exactly that intervention.

The whole plan

Apply every proposal you want to send, then in the sidebar click Share entire plan (all proposals). The link preserves their order and their dependencies.

A second variant

Want to try an alternative? Fork the proposal or create a replacement. The published original stays intact while the new version can evolve.

# Almaty general plan and detailed plans

The 2040 general plan is available in the planning institute's public map and as the adopted drawing attached to Decree No. 961. The institute serves the drawing as georeferenced images. Its vector tiles supply parcels and boundaries. The drawing tile metadata carries no decree number or revision date. The planning viewer takes vector area records, so the citywide drawing is available through the institute's map. [Institute map](https://map.almatygenplan.kz/) · [Tile metadata](https://map.almatygenplan.kz/t/genplan-raster.json) · [Decree No. 961](https://www.gov.kz/org/almaty-uaig/documents/details/1101671?lang=ru) · [Adopted main drawing, page 39](https://www.gov.kz/api/v1/public/assets/2026/11/17/392c19932ddc4d8bc9d5a286bcc0f059755214351c498c3e00426d9882f18529_original.1664161.pdf#page=39)

The city configuration selects **ПДП Алматы**, a snapshot of the state urban-planning cadastre taken on 24 November 2026. It uses the shared Kazakhstan reader and builder with layer `kz:pdpzone` and territory `750000000`. The snapshot has 1,856 records across three named detailed plans in southwest Almaty. **Fit plan** frames their combined extent. The companion detailed-plan red lines leave the approval number and date unrecorded. The national general-plan zone layer has no records for Almaty city. [Public cadastre](https://map.gov4c.kz/egkn/) · [Almaty detailed-plan layer](https://map.gov4c.kz/ggkgs/ows?service=WFS&version=2.0.0&request=GetFeature&typeNames=kz%3Apdpzone&CQL_FILTER=admterr_id%3D%27750000000%27&resultType=hits)

The detailed-plan polygons can be inspected by record ID and used as design sites. Permitted massing intersects the source zones with the configured municipal parcel source. The adapter reads the local functional label and uses bounded residential counts of 3, 5, 6, 9 or 12 storeys at an assumed 3 m per storey. For example, a record labelled 6–9 storeys gives 27 m even when its national classification is 6–12. The source supplies 94 polygons with a finite storey cap. Other polygons remain available for zoning inspection. Evidence shows the captured source properties and the basis of the derived height.

The detailed-plan overview and exact backend snapshot share the same source IDs. Rebuild them from the decompressed bundled GeoJSON.

```sh
node backend/scripts/build-kz-genplan-snapshot.mjs almaty-pdp-cadastre-2026-11-24 /path/to/captured-zones.geojson
```

## Digitizing the citywide drawing

The [drawing and polygon comparison](research_notes/almaty-vectorization/almaty-comparison.png) and [geographic polygons](research_notes/almaty-vectorization/almaty-sample.geojson) cover a square about 890 m across, centred on [43.229566° N, 76.881101° E](https://map.almatygenplan.kz/#16.8/43.229566/76.881101). The sample covers four land uses from the [institute’s legend](https://map.almatygenplan.kz/layers.json): mid-rise housing (Ж-3), multi-storey housing (Ж-4), housing above 12 storeys (Ж-5) and education (О-1). The polygons represent connected classified fills. Adjacent areas of the same use can merge across plan phases. The GeoJSON flags shapes clipped by the sample edge.

The sample uses a 512 × 512 pixel crop at zoom 16, about 1.74 m per pixel. Each pixel was compared with the dominant RGB colours of the official legend swatches. Nearby exposed colour helped classify dark building overprints. A nine-pixel closing operation bridged narrow gaps from lettering and hatching. Compact bright label holes surrounded by a single class were filled. Connected components below 100 pixels were dropped. The remaining pixel edges were traced and converted from the tile grid to longitude and latitude.

The four classes occupy 53.6% of the crop across 26 polygons. Grey in the comparison includes other land uses and unresolved pixels. Three education sites traced by hand on the same drawing gave intersection-over-union values of 86% (upper right), 92% (lower centre) and 83% (upper left). That measures agreement with a reading of the drawing. Surveyed boundary accuracy is unknown. Four multi-storey housing labels landed in gaps in the extracted polygons. Roads and overprints can split a zone, and several recreational legend entries share the same green. A broader extraction would need the printed codes and hatch patterns to distinguish those uses and phases.

### Reading labels and phases around Central Park

The [Central Park comparison](research_notes/almaty-vectorization/park-comparison.png) covers 1.11 km across at [43.2662° N, 76.9763° E](https://map.almatygenplan.kz/#17/43.2662/76.9763). The sample was selected for neighbouring areas of public green space (Р-1), parkland (Р-3), water and banks (Р-4), and education (О-1). The [GeoJSON](research_notes/almaty-vectorization/park-sample.geojson) includes the associated printed labels and coordinates, unresolved areas, and a flag for boundaries clipped by the sample. The [review points and hand traces](research_notes/almaty-vectorization/park-checks.json) use source-image pixel coordinates.

Matching the printed characters and their white halos against one example of each target code produced 51 additional automatic readings. Of those, 44 agreed with the 53 manually identified target labels beyond the four examples. Small differences between the digits 1 and 4 caused errors even where the colour was clear. The polygon assignment used the manually checked labels. A label needed a single compatible colour region occupying at least 32% of the ring 8–18 pixels around it, with at least 1.6 times the support of the runner-up. Of 41 candidate regions, 24 received a land use, 14 lacked a securely associated code, and three carried conflicting codes.

The education legend distinguishes existing sites by solid fill, first-stage sites by a grid and the plan horizon by diagonal hatching. Measuring horizontal, vertical and diagonal bright lines recovered all three styles. In 13 point checks where a phase was readable by eye, nine received the matching phase and four remained unresolved, including two points under a building symbol. Several points lie on the same site. The legend gives water and banks the same green in both published phases, so those polygons retain an unknown phase. Across all four uses, 11 of the 41 candidate regions received a phase.

Three independent hand outlines on the same drawing gave intersection-over-union values of 89% for a narrow park strip, 97% for a gridded education site and 92% for a diagonally hatched education area. The strip’s use was recovered while its phase remained unresolved. The sample supports producing draft outlines for review. Narrow strips, adjacent areas with the same fill and labels printed across boundaries still require a person to decide which polygon a code belongs to.

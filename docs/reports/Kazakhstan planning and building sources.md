# Kazakhstan planning and building sources

Alatau already has a useful official planning input, but it is only the first rung of a complete buildability model. The application reads the functional zones in the approved general plan and turns the residential labels into permitted storey and indicative height limits. Its companion red-line data can show the planned public street structure, but cannot yet calculate setbacks or buildable footprints because its published geometry has no documented edge convention or regulatory dimensions. The public cadastre exposes further planning, building and utility layers, although their Alatau completeness is uneven. For existing 3D buildings, no public, reusable authoritative Alatau model has been established: the regional digital twin and Alatau pilot are leads for a negotiated extract, while open/global sources provide only contextual 2.5D or OSM-derived representations.

## The two plan layers answer different questions

`kz:gpzone`, which supplies `alatau-genplan-727`, divides Alatau into functional areas such as detached, 2–3-storey, 4–6-storey and 6–12-storey housing. It records whether the intended use is existing or planned. The app currently translates the residential labels into a status and storey/height constraints; it deliberately does not infer setbacks, coverage or density, because the general plan does not publish them per parcel ([Alatau source contract](../alatau-genplan-source.md)).

`kz:gpreg` is the companion **red-line** layer. Kazakhstan’s Construction Code defines red lines as the boundaries between blocks or microdistricts and streets, access roads or squares; they regulate where the public street-side structure begins ([Construction Code](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/K2600000253/compare/eng)). The public WFS describes the Alatau records as `Красные линии`, tied to Decree 727, while the planning rules distinguish red/yellow lines and building-regulation lines as detailed-plan material ([planning WFS](https://map.gov4c.kz/ggkgs/ows?service=WFS&request=GetCapabilities&version=2.0.0), [planning-project rules](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2600038632)).

The distinction matters: functional zones say **what kind of development belongs in an area**; red lines describe **the public framework within which blocks and streets are organised**. `gpreg` can therefore become an excellent visible evidence layer, and later a constraint source. But its published MultiPolygons do not state whether their outer edge is a road reservation, a buffered boundary or a block-side area, nor do they carry road width, a building line or setback. We should validate that convention against Decree 727’s drawing before clipping a parcel envelope or generating a street from it.

## The public cadastre offers more evidence than rules

The government’s map service advertises parcels, free plots, land-value zones, cadastral quarters, agricultural/soil data, master and detailed plans, building records, orthophotos, duty plans, satellite-monitoring results and utility-service areas ([service description](https://www.gov.kz/services/4009?lang=ru)). Its planning WFS currently exposes functional zones and red lines for both general and detailed plans, a `building_blocks` footprint layer, and electricity, gas, heat, telephone, water and sewer-service layers ([WFS capabilities](https://map.gov4c.kz/ggkgs/ows?service=WFS&request=GetCapabilities&version=2.0.0)).

The current Alatau inventory is **6,659** `gpzone` functional areas, **2,691** `gpreg` red-line records and **zero** public `pdpzone`/`pdpreg` records. The zero concerns this public WFS only: it does not prove detailed plans have never been approved, digitised elsewhere or withheld from the public service. The Ministry says the national system has received hundreds of detailed plans for partial digital completion, which supports treating availability as a source-by-source question ([Ministry update](https://www.gov.kz/org/mps/press/news/details/1185067?lang=ru)). `building_blocks` is a promising official footprint source because its schema allows factual/project height and storey fields, but sampled Alatau-area records left most such attributes null. It is not yet an authoritative local 3D source. Utility layers need a coverage and attribute audit before they influence feasibility claims.

## Existing 3D sources divide into three classes

| Source | What it can supply now | Alatau status and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Almaty Region digital twin / Alatau pilot | Government 2D/3D maps, terrain and infrastructure work; a prospective city-specific twin | No verified public API, download, licence or building-mesh coverage. The pilot remains bounded and is a data-partnership lead, not a ready source ([region](https://www.gov.kz/org/almobl-enbek/press/news/details/676515?directionId=4419&lang=ru), [Alatau pilot](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/alatau-city-project_wsce2026-alatauonline-alataucity-activity-7503425944389595137-SpRg)). |
| GlobalBuildingAtlas | Individual-building LoD1 extrusions and ML-predicted heights across global coverage | The strongest open height candidate, but its Alatau WFS slice was not available for confirmation. LoD1/height data are CC BY-NC 4.0, so it needs a licence decision before product use ([publication](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6647/2025/essd-17-6647-2025.html), [licence](https://github.com/zhu-xlab/GlobalBuildingAtlas)). |
| Overture, Microsoft and Cesium OSM Buildings | Open/downloadable footprints, optional height/floors, or OSM-based 3D Tiles | Microsoft confirms footprints over the northern Alatau test area but no corresponding height tile. Overture fields are optional; Cesium is a contextual OSM extrusion, neither an official measurement ([Microsoft](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints/blob/main/README.md), [Overture](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/buildings/), [Cesium](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-osm-buildings/)). |

Astana has official evidence of a GIS platform with photorealistic 3D maps, and the national State Urban Planning Cadastre advertises a geoportal and per-building 3D-model service. Neither yielded a currently verified public viewer, export endpoint or Alatau coverage in this review ([Astana](https://www.gov.kz/org/astana-urgsa/activities/13241), [State Urban Planning Cadastre](https://aisggk.kz/)). Google’s country table concerns its Maps 3D SDK and does not establish whether Photorealistic 3D Tiles cover a particular Alatau point; that requires a location test with the Map Tiles API ([Maps 3D coverage](https://developers.google.com/maps/coverage), [Photorealistic 3D Tiles](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/3d-tiles-overview)).

## A small official data request is more valuable than another backdrop

For the presentation, retain the official parcel and `gpzone` evidence, add red lines only as a labelled plan layer after checking their geometry against the decree, and keep any OSM, Overture or Cesium buildings visually separate as existing-context data. An Overture sample can establish whether it adds useful footprints or heights before it replaces the existing OSM route. GlobalBuildingAtlas merits a visual trial only after its non-commercial licence and local slice are cleared.

The strategic request is a bounded Alatau extract from the regional twin or the City Authority’s pilot: building geometry and height, terrain, infrastructure and update metadata, with CRS, vertical datum, accuracy/LoD, object identity and explicit reuse terms. That would make the platform a place where authoritative current ground, official planning constraints and proposed alternatives meet, rather than treating a generic 3D backdrop as regulatory evidence.

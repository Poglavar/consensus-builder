# Adapting to the audience

Use the user's goals to choose the output. These are possible emphases, not assumptions about what a group wants.

| Audience | Useful emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Planners and urban designers | Spatial coherence, alternatives, confirmed controls, capacity, movement, deliverable drawings and rationale |
| Academics | Explicit hypotheses, reproducible methods and inputs, scenario parameters, uncertainty and limitations |
| NGOs | Access, inclusion, environmental outcomes, distribution of benefits and burdens, evidence for advocacy |
| Neighbourhood organizations | Plain language, local knowledge, understandable maps, everyday effects, recorded concerns and choices |
| City councils | Decision options, public-service needs, infrastructure, phasing, evidence behind policy fit and delivery assumptions |
| Real estate developers | Programme, site capacity, built form, access and servicing, delivery dependencies, transparent feasibility assumptions |

For a mixed group, provide a common map and comparison table, then explain the implications for different participants. Separate actual consultation findings from proposed engagement questions. Do not claim consensus without evidence of the relevant participants' views.
